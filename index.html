Hazard Gentrification & Environmental Justice

This project explores how historical redlining spatially aligns with race, income, education, and natural hazard exposure in Seattle, Washington.

The research evaluates whether low-income and minority populations are disproportionately distributed in natural hazard zones and whether those communities may face increased risk of hazard gentrification and displacement.

The project also investigates whether changes in ACS census data between the 2023 and 2024 5-year estimates display patterns associated with gentrification in historically redlined areas.

Research Questions

  • How does historical redlining spatially align with the demographic distribution of race, income, and education in Seattle?
  • Are low-income and minority demographics distributed in natural hazard zones, and could this increase vulnerability to hazard gentrification?
  • Do ACS demographic changes between 2023 and 2024 suggest gentrification patterns within historically redlined areas?

LiDAR Analysis & Certifications

In these models I began to clearly visualize areas that may be susceptible to landslides due to cracks, depressions, and terrain shading along the coast of Mercer Island and near the I-90 highway corridor.

The final result map contains four map views illustrating different approaches to landslide susceptibility analysis using LiDAR-derived terrain data.

The DEM landslide risk model was created by reclassifying elevation values to identify areas at or above 500 as potentially susceptible to landslides based on findings from previous LiDAR landslide studies.

The LiDAR slope model highlights lighter terrain areas that may indicate higher landslide susceptibility along the I-90 corridor and the outer portions of Mercer Island.

The LiDAR hillshade model reveals depressions, cracks, and terrain irregularities that may correspond with potential landslide activity.

The final landslide risk map incorporates City of Mercer Island landslide risk data, showing documented landslide locations and potential hazard zones.

Together, the map views illustrate that the areas most susceptible to landslides are concentrated along the I-90 corridor and the outer slopes surrounding Mercer Island.
