MA in Geography Thesis, Mapping Compounding Climate Displacement Vulnerability in Washington State: A Proposed Environmental Justice Framework

Abstract

Climate change is increasing the frequency of natural hazards and the risk of migration and displacement for vulnerable populations. Despite growing environmental justice (EJ) research on unequal exposure, limited studies examine the compounding exposure of climate displacement vulnerability, gentrification pressure, and public health burdens through place-based indicators.

This study develops a Climate Displacement Social Vulnerability (CDSV) index and related spatial indices for identifying compounding climate displacement vulnerability through the interaction of social vulnerability, neighborhood change, hazard exposure, and public health burdens in Washington State. Integrated demographic, socioeconomic, neighborhood, and accessibility indicators are analyzed using critical GIS, statistical correlation, and ordinary least squares (OLS) regression methods.

Results indicate compounding spatial exposure to climate displacement vulnerability, gentrification pressure, and health burdens in Washington. The proposed methodology and GIS framework are presented as a replicable approach for future place based EJ assessments across the United States and other vulnerable regions.

Hazard Gentrification & Environmental Justice

This project evaluates how historical redlining spatially aligns with race, income, education, and natural hazard exposure across Seattle, Washington.

The research investigates whether low-income and minority populations are disproportionately distributed in natural hazard zones and whether those communities may face increased risk of hazard gentrification and climate-driven displacement.

ACS demographic estimates, historical HOLC redlining data, and Seattle hazard exposure datasets were integrated within a GIS workflow to analyze spatial patterns of vulnerability, inequality, and neighborhood change.
